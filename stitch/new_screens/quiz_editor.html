Consider the following statements regarding the Basic Structure Doctrine in Indian Constitutional Jurisprudence:

  1. It was first explicitly articulated by the Supreme Court in the I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab (1967) ruling.
  2. Judicial review and the secular character of the Indian Republic form non-derogable parts of the basic structure as upheld in the Minerva Mills case.
  3. The doctrine limits Parliament’s constituent amending power under Article 368 from emasculating fundamental guarantees.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Pedagogical Explanation & Academic Citations

Revealed post-submission
Model Editorial Solution Target: IAS Prelims Standard

The doctrine of ‘Basic Structure’ of the Constitution was established by a landmark 7:6 majority in the historic Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) case, overruling the earlier absolute curtailment in Golaknath (1967). In the subsequent Minerva Mills case (1980), the Court reiterated that judicial review, harmony between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles, and limited amending capacity are constituent elements of the basic structure.
